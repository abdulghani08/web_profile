# Audience and Personas

These are working personas for design and content decisions, not claims about specific real individuals.

## Persona A — Recruiter

**Goal:** Quickly assess role fit and find credible experience.  
**Needs:** Clear headline, core skills, experience timeline, selected projects, contact/profile links.  
**Concern:** Inflated claims or unclear role ownership.  
**Design response:** Make positioning and experience scannable; use accurate dates and concise evidence.

## Persona B — Engineering lead / hiring manager

**Goal:** Evaluate practical engineering experience and reasoning.  
**Needs:** Specific project context, personal contribution, technical decisions where safe to share, constraints, and verifiable outcomes.  
**Concern:** A portfolio that lists technologies without demonstrating use.  
**Design response:** Prioritize a small number of evidence-rich case studies; separate confirmed facts from unavailable details.

## Persona C — Potential client

**Goal:** Understand whether the owner can solve a relevant problem.  
**Needs:** Types of systems delivered, collaboration approach, project examples, clear contact route.  
**Concern:** Unclear scope or overpromising.  
**Design response:** Explain practical capabilities and invite a conversation without guaranteeing results.

## Persona D — International / remote opportunity

**Goal:** Understand the profile in English and navigate the site easily.  
**Needs:** Natural English copy, consistent dates/terminology, clear location/time context when relevant.  
**Concern:** Literal or inconsistent translations.  
**Design response:** Review both language versions independently.

## Design implications

- The first screen should communicate identity and relevance quickly.
- Use evidence before decorative claims.
- Provide multiple routes to the same important content without clutter.
- Do not prioritize client-sales copy over the approved 80% technical / 20% leadership emphasis.
