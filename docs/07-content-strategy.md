# Content Strategy

## Principles

- Lead with software development, not a broad generic “IT professional” label.
- Make the content useful to recruiters and technical reviewers within a short scan.
- Prefer a few strong case studies over a long, shallow project gallery.
- Use concrete verbs: built, maintained, integrated, implemented, investigated, coordinated—only when supported by the source.
- Do not invent results or turn approximate figures into precise measurements.
- Preserve role attribution: individual contribution, team contribution, and organization-level outcomes must be distinguishable.

## Content hierarchy

1. Name and positioning
2. Brief professional summary
3. Selected projects / case studies
4. Development skills and technologies
5. Relevant experience
6. Supporting systems/ERP and leadership experience
7. Contact and professional profiles

## Case-study content template

- Title and concise summary
- Context / problem
- Intended users or operational need (only if safe to disclose)
- Role and responsibilities
- Contribution and implementation
- Technology used (verified)
- Constraints and trade-offs (only if known)
- Outcome and evidence
- Links/media (verified and permitted)
- Confidentiality note if needed

If an element is unknown, omit it or label it as information awaiting confirmation during drafting. Do not fill it with plausible-sounding content.

## Bilingual workflow

- Maintain one source of truth for facts, metrics, names, dates, and technology names.
- Draft and review English and Indonesian versions for natural language.
- Keep essential content equivalent across languages.
- Language switch must preserve the visitor's current page when a translation exists; fallback behavior is a design/technical decision.
- Review spelling, terminology, dates, and external links in both versions.

## Content maintenance

Keep copy and project data separate from presentation where practical. Final implementation format depends on the approved stack and architecture.
